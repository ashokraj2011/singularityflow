# {{initiative.id}} — {{output.label}}

The announcement as it would read on the day this ships, written before it is built. The discipline
is that a release which cannot be described in plain terms to the people it is for has usually not
been understood well enough to build.

## Press release

Written in the past tense, as though it has already launched. If this paragraph is hard to write,
that is the finding.

### Headline

One line. What is now possible that was not.

### Sub-headline

Who it is for and the benefit, in one sentence.

### The problem

The situation before, from the customer's side rather than the organisation's.

### The solution

What was built, in the terms a customer would use.

### Quote from us

Why we did this, said by someone who would actually be quoted.

### How it works

The shortest honest description of getting from problem to benefit.

### Quote from a customer

What a satisfied user would say. If the only plausible quote is about the organisation rather than
their own outcome, the benefit may not be where it was assumed.

### Availability

Who can get it, from when, and how.
