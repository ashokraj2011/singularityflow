## 1. Decision

**When a clause changes, SFlow already keeps the evidence of unaffected clauses. This specification closes the gaps that stop that from being sound and usable on its own: dependencies are declared, not observed; an unknown dependency is treated as no dependency; the step an amendment reopens can still publish what it drafted against the old scope; and a person cannot see the exact effect of a change before deciding it.**

Nothing here adds a store, a lifecycle or an approval path. It adds records to the existing Story state, one check at the existing publication boundary, and one read-only preview in front of the existing intent-amendment route. The preview and the approval compute the effect with one function, so what a person previews is what approving does.

## 3. Invariants

| ID | Invariant |
| --- | --- |
| CIC:CON-001 | The cone only grows from observation. Recorded dependencies add to declared and cited ones; they never remove one. |
| CIC:CON-002 | Missing information widens the cone. No model statement, and no absence of a record, narrows it. |
| CIC:CON-003 | The only routes that keep evidence inside the cone without re-running its step are the approver "still holds" answer for that step and generation, and a change the scope approvers approved as `editorial` (D2), which the record says was carried that way. |
| CIC:CON-004 | Exact phase-bundle approval remains the only approval that advances lifecycle state (SDS:CON-061). This specification adds no clause-level approval. |
| CIC:CON-005 | Work superseded by a revision is retained as history. It is never deleted, relabelled or published. |
| CIC:CON-006 | The preview is read-only: it records no history entry and writes no file under the Story. Every mutation continues through the existing amendment, change-request and return routes. |
| CIC:CON-007 | The preview and the approval compute the effect with one function, from the same Story state and proposed bytes, bound to the scope generation the amendment was proposed against. A preview of a proposal the Story moved under says so instead of showing an effect approval would refuse. |

## 4. Requirements

### 4.1 Observed dependencies (G1)

**[CIC:REQ-001]** When a step publishes a generation, the kernel MUST record a dependency record for it from production facts only: the clause revisions (ID and statement hash) in its composed input as the prompt receipt records them (under switch 1 the receipt records them; before stage 3 it held only the capsule's digest and counts); the clause claims in the planned claim maps (a code step's observed claim map is written at submission, after the record, so its claims are the ones planned for it); the paths in its code-delivery change set; the tests bound to its clauses by the traceability bindings; the witnesses; and the source and implementation bindings and the citations. A model's description of what it used is not an input.

**[CIC:REQ-002]** Dependency records MUST be append-only, digest-bound to the step generation and to the scope revision current at publication, and kept in the existing Story state. Each edge names its kind: `input` (in the composed input), `claim` (the generation claims the clause), `path` (it changed a path a clause expects), `test` (a bound test verifies the clause), `witness`, `binding` or `citation`. Each revision recorded after enablement is a `scope-revision/v2` record that stores the stale set it computed, with each clause's reason (`added`, `revised`, or `depends` with the clauses it depends through), and the edges it used; v1 records are never rewritten, and the hash chain runs through both and is verified on read. A broken chain or a tampered record is reported and read as `unresolved`; no refusal is added, since none existed for v1.

**[CIC:REQ-003]** The effective dependency set used for staleness MUST be the union of `dependsOn`, exact-ID citations and recorded edges. `staleClausesOf` and `scopeStaleness` keep their current results as a lower bound; recorded edges can only add stale items.

**[CIC:REQ-004]** For a revised clause, the cone MUST include the file-level closure: the paths claimed for that clause, their transitive dependents from the incremental verification graph, and any evidence whose witness covers one of those files. Evidence is addressed by clause, step and generation as today.

### 4.2 Widening (G2)

**[CIC:REQ-005]** Each step generation MUST have a coverage state: `resolved` when a complete dependency record exists for it, otherwise `unresolved`. Generations published before enablement, generations whose receipt or claim map is missing or fails its digest, and generations that changed a path no clause claims are `unresolved`. A changed path that an approved plan row or approved plan amendment (`PAM-NNN`, add-supporting) names as a supporting change counts as covered and depends on no clause (owner decision, 2026-11-14). Each unresolved generation names its reasons from a closed set (section 7.2).

**[CIC:REQ-006]** On any revision that adds, revises or removes a clause, every `unresolved` generation of the defining step and of every later step MUST be treated as inside the cone: an unresolved record counts as affected, never as unaffected. A removed clause counts as changed for the clauses that depend on it. A revision with `changes: null` (the first revision, or one from before E6) makes every clause it holds stale. A changed clause that no plan claims reaches every later published step whose record does not resolve it. A revision reaches a step's evidence at a generation as follows: a step it lists in `dependentGenerations`, at or below the generation recorded there, generation 0 included once the step has published (a step that never published holds no evidence, so a Story's first revision marks nothing stale for the steps after its scope); the step whose generation made the revision, at its earlier generations; and any other step, at every generation not published after the revision (by its `generationPublications` time; an unknown time counts as before).

**[CIC:REQ-007]** Every item in a computed cone MUST carry the reason it is there: `declared`, `cited`, `recorded:<edge kind>`, `file-closure`, `unreadable` or `widened:unresolved`. The share of the cone that is widened is reported, so teams can see what missing records cost them.

**[CIC:REQ-020]** A missing or unreadable artifact of a step after the scope MUST count as affected by an approved amendment, so its step is not kept by rule E8. An artifact that was recorded as absent and is still absent holds no evidence and is neither affected nor kept. This closes G2 place (2) and is delivered without a switch: it only resets a step whose evidence cannot be read.

**[CIC:REQ-021]** A document step that rule E8 would keep because its artifacts cite none of the changed clauses, while those clauses were in its prompt, MUST be asked "still holds" by its own approvers on the decisions card: one question per such step per change (D1). Until it is answered the step's evidence of those clauses is stale; "returns" reopens it through the existing route.

### 4.3 The reopened step (G3)

**[CIC:REQ-008]** When an approved amendment reopens a step in place, the kernel MUST set aside that step's pending prompt pair with the amendment as its reason (`supersedePromptGeneration`), close its open generation intent, and pin the scope revision the step now works against.

**[CIC:REQ-009]** At publication, a generation composed against an earlier scope revision than the step's pin MUST be refused with a typed code naming the revision and the amendment; it is not rebased (owner default). The refusal's route composes the step again on the current scope.

**[CIC:REQ-010]** The mutation guard MUST NOT import the check. A code step's application writes from the draft are denied because REQ-008 closes its generation, which the guard already requires open; the denial returns the step's re-entry route (`phase begin`). A document step's writes are how it is written again, so they stay allowed, and REQ-009 refuses an agent's publication of it until the step is composed again. Reads remain allowed. Work already produced is retained under CON-005. (Built in stage 5 without the flag first planned here: section 7.4.)

**[CIC:REQ-011]** A step rule E8 keeps is not touched: its approval and its published generation stand, and nothing is set aside.

### 4.4 Preview, class and allowance (G4)

**[CIC:REQ-012]** `singularity-flow story intent-amendment preview` MUST be a model-free read, registered as a read, that computes without recording anything: for a drafted amendment (`--file`), what proposing it records (clause diff, radius) and what approving it would do; for a pending proposal (`<AMD-ID>`), what approving it does now. The effect it shows is the one approval applies (CON-007): the stale clauses with their dependents; for each later step, whether it is reset (and which one is reopened first), kept, or asked "still holds", each with its reason (a path in the radius, the clauses it cites, a code step the radius reaches, a convergence step, an unreadable artifact, or kept because it cites none, with "the clause was in its prompt" shown as information); the evidence kept; the plan rows carried; the approver groups asked "still holds"; and the churn and reversals of D4. Its agent view stays within 4,000 bytes; the whole result is kept.

**[CIC:REQ-013]** Each proposed change MUST be classified by registered rules as `editorial`, `refinement` or `material`. A model MAY propose the class; the rules bound it. A change is not `editorial` if it adds or removes a clause, changes a clause with a bound test or an acceptance clause, or changes expected paths or test bindings. A change that adds a clause, widens expected paths beyond the Story's admitted scope, or changes required authority is `material`. An ambiguous change takes the stricter class.

**[CIC:REQ-014]** Class determines handling, within CON-004. An `editorial` change carries the evidence of the clause it rewords without per-step "still holds": the scope approvers approve the new wording as editorial, and the approval record says the evidence was carried that way (D2). A `refinement` follows the existing intent-amendment route with the computed cone and its rows. A `material` change additionally needs the scope-change authority: an optional policy key naming an `approvalAuthorities` group (`spec.scopeChangeAuthority`), defaulting to the scope step's own approval groups.

**[CIC:REQ-015]** A person MUST be able to state a change in plain language in the Story's chat. The governed skill (`/sf-reject`) drafts it, shows the preview in the person's terms (what changes, what is redone, what is kept, who must confirm) and then proposes it through the existing route. No new mutation route and no new stop is added.

**[CIC:REQ-016]** No default change allowance is set (D4). The preview and the Story report MUST show the amendment churn and any clause reversed more than once. A pinned policy MAY later set an allowance once real Stories have been measured.

### 4.5 Measurement and enablement

**[CIC:REQ-017]** Per Story, counts MUST record revisions by class, cone size, widened share, evidence items kept, "still holds" questions and answers, steps reset and publications refused. Content is not recorded.

**[CIC:REQ-018]** The capability MUST be enabled per workflow by pinned policy in three independent switches (since 2026-11-15, switches 1 and 3 are on where a workflow does not set `spec.changeImpact`: section 9a, decision 11), following the `spec` block pattern and read from the pinned raw field: switch 1 (`spec.observedDependencies: on`), observed dependencies, widening, file closure and D1 (REQ-001 to 007, 021); switch 2, the reopened step's pin and the publication check (REQ-008 to 011); switch 3, classes, the scope-change authority and the chat entry (REQ-013 to 016), which `spec.changeImpact: on` turns on together with switch 1 as one setting (section 7.6). The preview (REQ-012) and the fail-closed artifact read (REQ-020) need no switch: one only shows, the other only resets a step whose evidence cannot be read. With all switches off, behaviour is that of the source baseline plus REQ-020.

**[CIC:REQ-019]** Records added by this specification MUST be additive to the existing Story state, and no migration rewrites earlier records (D3). Widening of earlier generations under REQ-006 applies only to Stories whose pinned policy enables switch 1; a Story pinned before enablement keeps baseline behaviour, plus REQ-020.

### 7.1 What stage 2 built, and what building it found

- `intentAmendmentEffect` (`src/state.mjs`) computes the effect from the Story as it is and the proposed bytes, before anything changes; `decideIntentAmendment` computes it at the same point and applies it, and `story intent-amendment preview`, the decisions card (terminal, VS Code Decide card, Copilot CLI card) and `/sf-reject` show it. The card's "Steps it may reopen" (every later started step) is replaced by the effect, step by step.
- The stale set is read from the scope revision the approval would record (`nextScopeRevision`, `src/scope/revisions.mjs`), built from the amended scope's clauses as its index will read them; the approval still records the revision after the index is refreshed, and the acceptance test pins the two equal.
- One case resets more than the preview shows, never less: a plan whose rows the amendment carries is kept only if it can read the amended scope once it exists (`carriedPlanBinding`, after the bytes are written). The preview shows it kept; if it cannot read it, approval runs it again with every step after it.
- REQ-020's unreadable case cannot be reached through the CLI for a file in the Story: every governed write first copies the Story directory to roll back from (`src/publication-recovery.mjs`), and an unreadable file there refuses the write. The engine's own check is the second line, and the test runs it in process.
- A step that never ran is reset in the effect's record but not named in its words: it reads the amended scope when it starts.
- Churn and reversals (D4) are read from the Story's approved amendments and its scope revisions' statement hashes.

### 7.2 What stage 3 built, and what building it found

- **Switch 1** is `spec.observedDependencies: on | off` in the workflow definition's `spec` block (`schemas/workflow-definition.schema.json`, `normalizeSpecPolicy`). It is pinned only when on, so a Story without it (every Story pinned before it, and every new Story while it is off) keeps byte-identical state; readers check the pinned raw field. With it off nothing new is written: no record, no `dependencyRecords`, v1 revisions, and receipts as before.
- **Records** (`src/dependency-records.mjs`, loaded only on the publish path and by the read). `publishGeneration` writes one per generation after the generation's own records are final, in the publication's commit; `decideIntentAmendment` writes the record of the scope generation it publishes. One file per generation keeps `workflow.json` small: the step holds only `{ generation, path, recordSha256 }`. On the fixture the records were 976 to 2,427 bytes. Each `input` edge costs about 160 bytes, so a step after 100 clauses carries about 17 KB, in its own file.
- **Prompt receipts did not name the clause revisions.** They held the capsule's digest and counts only. Under switch 1 the composer now records `clauseRevisions` (each ID and approved statement hash, the same hash a scope revision holds) on the receipt. A receipt exists only when a prompt was composed (`phase enter --compose`, an agent's entry). A generation authored without one, such as deterministic code or the scope generation an amendment publishes, reads `unresolved` with `receipt-missing`. That will be common, and it is the fail-closed reading REQ-005 asks for. (Stage 7 corrects this for the two routes that compose no prompt by design: section 7.7.)
- **Coverage reasons**, a closed set. At publication: `receipt-missing`, `receipt-digest` (the prompt no longer matches the receipt), `receipt-clauses-unrecorded`, `claim-map-missing`, `claim-map-digest`, `delivery-evidence-missing`, `unclaimed-paths` and `artifact-unreadable`. On read: `no-record`, `record-missing`, `record-digest`, `receipt-changed`, `claim-map-changed` and `scope-chain`.
- **Changed paths no clause claims:** under REQ-005 as first written, a plan's Supporting files (lockfiles, CI, build files) were unclaimed, so most real code generations that touch one read `unresolved`. The owner decided (2026-11-14) that a supporting change an approved plan row or plan amendment names is covered; stage 4 records it as `supportingPaths`.
- **`scope-revision/v2`** is written by `recordApprovedScopeRevision` under switch 1, with `stale` (each clause's reason) and `edges` (the declared edges the reasons used). Staleness still reads `changes` and `dependsOn` exactly as before, and the test pins `stale` equal to `staleClausesOf`. `schemas/workflow.schema.json` takes each item as v1 or v2, both with `additionalProperties: false`.
- **Nothing verified the v1 chain before.** `verifyScopeRevisionChain` checks each revision's digest and its link to the one before, through v1 and v2. Only the read uses it, and a record whose revision is not in a chain that verifies reads `unresolved` with `scope-chain`.
- **Outcomes are unchanged.** On the fixture, the same Story with the switch on and off has equal amendment effects, revision changes, staleness, reset and kept steps, and history.
- **Publish time:** the CLI's `phase publish` took 1.7 s, 1.7 s and 2.9 s for intake, fix-design and implement with the switch on, and 1.7 s, 1.7 s and 2.8 s with it off, within run-to-run noise.

### 7.3 What stage 4 built, and what building it found

Everything below applies only to a Story whose pinned spec policy has `observedDependencies: on`. With the switch off, outcomes are those of main: the stage 2 and stage 3 acceptance tests and the rest of the suite run with it off.

- **Staleness (REQ-006, `scope/revisions.mjs`).** Three rules close the remaining fail-open places of G2: a clause that depends on a removed one is stale; a revision with `changes: null` makes every clause it holds stale; and neither a step the revision does not list nor generation 0 is exempt. The reach rule is the one written in REQ-006. `scope-revision/v2` states the same set per clause, with the reasons `depends` (through a removed clause too) and `unrecorded`.
- **The amendment's effect** (`intentAmendmentEffect`, used by the preview, the card and the approval alike) reads each published step's record as it reads now. A step whose record is missing or unresolved is affected, with the reason `unresolved-record` and its reasons, so rule E8 never keeps it. Changed clauses no plan claims are listed as `unclaimedClauses`.
- **D1 (REQ-021).** A step rule E8 keeps whose record holds an `input` edge for a changed clause (one its prompt held and its document does not cite) gets a row on the Story's revalidation list: the amendment's `revalidate`, with `reason: prompt-held`, the clauses, and a recommendation. The recommendation is "still holds" when the step's document names neither those clauses nor any path they expect, otherwise "redo" (return the Story to it).
  - The card says why in one line, on the terminal, the VS Code Decide card and the Copilot CLI card alike.
  - The row is never settled for the person: the "settled for you" route does not take revalidation items.
  - It is answered on the Decide card or in the person's own terminal (`upstream-issue revalidate`), and since 2026-11-14 also as a yes or no in a /sf-decide chat (section 9a, decision 3).
- **Where an unanswered question waits:**
  - the approval of the step it is about, and of each step that reads it as an input;
  - for a step that completes at its submission (approval `none`, or a waiver), that submission;
  - the Story's end.

  Nothing else waits, and nothing loops back. The refusal is `AMENDMENT_STILL_HOLDS_REQUIRED` (person-decides-in-step), and it names the Decide card (`/sf-decide`) and the approvers' own command. The decision list marks the row as holding an approval only where the engine says so. `test/no-dead-end.test.mjs` walks this route (`code-step-waits-for-still-holds`).
- **Found:** a kept document step usually reads the scope only through its prompt's clause capsule, not as a phase input, so it has no input record. The existing "still holds" path refused it ("cannot read the approved scope"). For a D1 row the answer is now bound to the amended scope generation and its bytes, which is what carries its evidence across the revision (`revalidationCarriers`, `scopeStaleness`).
- **Found:** quick-fix's code step has approval `none` and completes at its submission. "Its own approval" therefore means its submission, which is where that step waits.
- **Supporting changes:** a changed path an approved plan row or plan amendment names as supporting is covered (`supportingPaths`), depends on no clause, and leaves the record resolved.

### 7.4 What stage 5 built, and what building it found

- **Switch 2** is `spec.preAmendmentDrafts: refuse | legacy` in the workflow definition's `spec` block. Unlike switch 1, it is pinned for every new Story, as `refuse` unless the workflow says `legacy` (owner, 2026-11-14). A Story pinned before the field has no value, reads as `legacy`, and keeps main's behaviour. Readers check the pinned raw field.
- **What approval does (REQ-008, `src/amended-drafts.mjs`, loaded only by the approval, the publication and the composer).** `decideIntentAmendment` pins the step it reopens in place to the amended scope as `phase.scopePin`: the amendment, the generation, and the scope revision and its digest. Then:
  - it sets aside the step's pending prompt pair for that generation, with the amendment as the reason (`supersedePromptGeneration`);
  - it closes the step's open code generation as `superseded`, keeping its start receipt beside the draft;
  - it copies the step's draft, read only, to `context/superseded/<step>-gen<N>-before-<AMD>/`.
- **Records, not times (REQ-009).** Under switch 2 each code generation's start receipt (`generation-start`) and each composed prompt's receipt record `scopeRevision`: the latest revision, its digest, and the last approved amendment. The reopened generation publishes only if both its code generation and its composed prompt started under the pinned revision or a later one, compared by the revision's place in `scopeRevisions` by digest. A document step an agent may have written (`governed-agent`, or no `--authored`) must also have been composed again since the pin, since an agent reads the amended scope only through that prompt. Its text may be the kept draft's unchanged: a person who finds it still holds publishes it as is, as their own, and its approvers approve it again (owner, 2026-11-14; section 9a, decision 5). As first built, unchanged bytes were refused; that check is gone. The refusal is `PHASE_DRAFT_PREDATES_AMENDMENT` (author-repairs-in-step). It names the amendment, the revision, the read-only draft, and the routes in the step, in order: `phase begin <step>` for a closed code generation, then `phase enter <step> --compose`. Nothing loops back and nothing else waits. `test/no-dead-end.test.mjs` walks it (`draft-written-before-the-amendment`): publish refused, begin, compose, publish.
- **Found: the closed generation's code.** It is written inside governance, but with the generation closed it read as code changed before the first `phase begin`. Two consequences followed:
  - the dirty-start rule blocked the new begin;
  - the step's entry sent its files to a person to set aside.

  Now a `phase begin` at the same generation and baseline carries that code (`baseline.mode: carried`, `carriedFrom`). The entry scopes it from the closed generation's baseline (`basis: closed-generation`). Protected and out-of-boundary paths still refuse as before.
- **REQ-010 needs no guard flag.** The guard already denies a code step's application edits without an open generation, and returns `phase begin`. Closing the generation at approval is therefore the flag, and the guard imports nothing new (its closure stays at 68 modules). A document step's artifact edits are how it is written again, so denying them would remove the way out. The publication check covers that step instead. REQ-010 and AC-009 are restated to match.
- **The old draft, read only, in the recomposed prompt (REQ-008).** The reopened step's next composed prompt carries a mandatory section, "Draft written before AMD-NNN (read only)". It names the copy's exact path, says it was written before the amendment, and says to keep from it only what still holds under the amended scope. It adds 357 to 368 bytes on the fixtures, against an 18,000-token (72,000-byte) prompt limit, and only on the pinned generation.
- **Found: a later reset step can hold a draft too.** When the first reset step comes before the current step, the current step's draft is reset with it. That step is pinned the same way when it still holds an open code generation or a composed prompt. In practice its code also blocks the earlier document step's publication (an artifact-only step), on main as well, until a person sets the code aside.
- **REQ-011:** a step rule E8 keeps is never pinned, and nothing of it is set aside.
- **Hosts:** the refusal reaches no card. It is the publish refusal's plan, shown alike in the terminal, VS Code Copilot and the Copilot CLI, and its routes pass the command crosswalk: `/sf-code` for `phase begin`, the entry's own route for `phase enter --compose`.
- **Middle states are compared, not only ends.** On the fixtures the same Story is walked with the switch on and off, and compared at each point:
  - after scope approval;
  - with the code step drafted;
  - after the amendment is approved;
  - after acknowledgement;
  - before and after the reopened step is published.

  At each point the steps' status and generation, the history, and the decision list are equal. Where the two legitimately differ is asserted explicitly: the pin, the closed generation, the set-aside prompt, the kept draft, the refusal, and the guard's answer.

### 7.5 Stage 5 follow-up: the reopened step is not stalled by a later step's code

- **The stall (section 7.4).** An amendment reopened Design while Implement held unfinished code. With that code in the working tree, Design could be neither checked, published, submitted nor approved (`PHASE_ARTIFACT_ONLY_CHANGES`, then `PHASE_SOURCE_CHANGED_AFTER_PUBLICATION` and `LIFECYCLE_WORKTREE_REVIEW_REQUIRED`) until a person set the code aside. Design stalled on Implement's work (section 9a, decision 6).
- **The record.** At the closure, `pinReopenedStep` records the closed generation's code as `scopePin.closedChangeSet`: the intent, its baseline commit, and each application path its change set reaches, with its kind and content digest (`missing` for a removal). The change set is the generation boundary's own (`buildRepositoryChangeSet`, `applicationChangeSetProjection`). Protected and out-of-boundary paths, and anything but a file or a link, are never recorded.
- **The exclusion (`withoutHeldCode`).** An earlier document step leaves out exactly the recorded paths whose bytes, read again from the same baseline, still match, while the code step's generation is still the closed one. It applies at prepublish and publication (`assertPhaseChangeScope`), at artifact registration (`scanArtifacts`: the code is never registered or committed with the document), at submission's after-publication check, at approval's clean-checkout check (the command, the selection receipt and the approval door), and in the recovery plan's worktree review, which admits composition.
- **Refused as before:** a recorded path changed since (the refusal says so and how to put it back), new code, protected and out-of-boundary paths, and anything with switch 2 off.
- **The carry agrees.** The code step's next `phase begin` compares its own change set, from the same baseline, with the record by the same path map. It carries exactly the record, and refuses a path changed since, or new code, in that step (`GENERATION_DIRTY_START` with `changedSinceClosure`). A closure from before the record carries as before.
- **The empty owner.** `describeCrossPhaseChange` ended "owned by ." when no earlier code step owned the paths; the phrase is now left out then. It is the one source of that sentence for `PHASE_ARTIFACT_ONLY_CHANGES`, `PHASE_SOURCE_CHANGED_AFTER_PUBLICATION` and `PRIOR_CODE_TEST_EVIDENCE_STALE`; the last always has an earlier code step.
- **The other routes back to an earlier document step:**
  - A "still holds" answered No (`upstream-issue revalidate … --outcome return`) while the amendment has the code step's generation closed: the closure's record covers it.
  - `upstream-issue decide … --outcome return`, and that No after the code step was begun again: the return (`rejectPhase`) leaves the code step's generation open and unpublished, so the record did not apply. The same mechanism now does: under switch 2 the return records that open generation's code as `phase.heldCode` with its change request, and the exclusion holds while that same generation stays open and unpublished. The code step goes on in it when the Story reaches it.
  - `reject --to <earlier>`: a code step is rejected only once submitted, so its code is published and committed. An edit made after its publication belongs to no generation, and is set aside as before.
  - `/sf-return` rebuilds a published Story on another machine from a clean checkout; it returns no step.
- **Tests.** `test/cic-pre-amendment-drafts.test.mjs` covers the record, exclusion and carry on a real checkout. It walks the Story with the switch on and off, compared after the amendment, before the design is written, and after it is published, submitted and approved, and after the code step publishes; on keeps the code in place and carries it, off sets it aside. It also covers the refusals (a changed path, new code, a protected path, at approval and at begin), the upstream-issue return on and off, and a "still holds" No. `test/no-dead-end.test.mjs` walks the design published with the code in place, then the code step published.

### 7.6 What stage 6 built, and what building it found

Everything below applies only to a Story whose pinned spec policy has `changeImpact: on`. With it off, and on every Story pinned before it, amendments carry no class and behave as on main: the stage 2 to 5 acceptance tests and the rest of the suite run with it off.

- **Switch 3 is one visible setting with switch 1:** `spec.changeImpact: on | off` in the workflow definition's `spec` block, on by default since 2026-11-15, applied in code (section 7.8; off through stage 6). On, it pins `changeImpact: on` and `observedDependencies: on` together; off, it pinned nothing, so a Story's pinned policy was byte-identical to one written before the field (since the default changed, off is pinned as `off`). `spec.observedDependencies: on` alone stays valid (switch 1 without classes), and `changeImpact: on` with `observedDependencies: off` is refused at load. Readers check the pinned raw field. `spec dependencies` says when change impact is on.
- **The rules (REQ-013, `src/amendment-class.mjs`, loaded only on a Story with the switch).** Nine registered rules, each a rule ID with a class: `adds-clause`, `widens-paths`, `changes-authority` and `bindings-unreadable` are material; `removes-clause`, `tested-clause`, `acceptance-clause` and `changes-bindings` are refinements; `wording-only` is editorial, when none of the others applies. The floor is the strictest reason. The inputs are model-free: the clause diff; the Story's merged planned and observed claims (a test the code step bound counts as much as a planned one); the rows the amendment carries for a separate plan (`keep-row` binds nothing new; every other row operation changes bindings); where the scope plans its own clauses, the amended text's own plan rows against the current ones; the paths the plan admits (planned expected paths and supporting files); and the protected paths of the governance and capability policies.
- **Found: "changes required authority" needed a model-free reading.** The scope's text cannot change an approval policy, which is pinned. The one way an amendment changes who must approve is a path it adds to the plan under a protected path, whose own approval then applies: that is `changes-authority`. "Widens expected paths beyond the Story's admitted scope" is a path it adds that no approved plan row or supporting file holds (`widens-paths`). Both are reached only through a carried row (`--add-row`, `--replace-location`) or a scope that plans its own clauses; an added clause is material anyway.
- **Ambiguity takes the stricter class:** when the observed claims or the amended text's plan cannot be read, `bindings-unreadable` makes the change material.
- **The record.** The proposal records `classification`: the floor, its reasons (rule ID, class, and the clauses, tests or paths), the proposer's suggestion (`suggested`: the class, who, and their authorship) and the effective class; the summary keeps `class`. Each approval records the class it approved and, when it raised it, `raise: { from, to }`. The approval's `application.class` keeps the floor, the rule IDs, the suggestion, each raise (who, from what, to what) and the effective class. Under the switch only; additive, so nothing earlier is rewritten (REQ-019).
- **Suggest, raise, never lower.** `--class` on `propose` and `preview --file` is the proposer's suggestion: a class below the floor is raised to it, and the preview and the card say "It was suggested as an editorial change; the rules raise it to a refinement", never refused (AC-013). `--class` on `decide` is an approver's raise, on an approval only; a lower class is refused with `INTENT_AMENDMENT_CLASS_LOWERED`, which names the approval at its class and each stricter one. The effective class is the strictest of the floor (recomputed at each read, never below the one recorded at proposal), the suggestion and every raise. On a Story without the switch, `--class` is refused with `INTENT_AMENDMENT_CLASS_UNAVAILABLE` and the command runs without it.
- **Found: a raise during approval.** With a minimum of two approvals, a second approver may raise the class after a first approved it. An approval recorded for a lower class than the amendment now has no longer counts toward it, and that approver may approve again (the distinct-identities rule counts only approvals that count).
- **Who decides (REQ-014).** `spec.scopeChangeAuthority` names an `approvalAuthorities` group (checked at load; valid only under `changeImpact: on`). A material change is approved by its members, as many as the scope step's minimum, under the scope step's self-approval rule; without the key, by the scope step's own groups. A material approval by anyone else is refused with `INTENT_AMENDMENT_MATERIAL_AUTHORITY`, which names the group, its members and both routes (the Decide card, `/sf-decide`, and the decide command in their own terminal); nothing is recorded, and rejecting stays the scope approvers' as before. An approver of a separate plan whose rows the amendment carries still signs those rows: that signs the plan, not the change. `test/no-dead-end.test.mjs` walks the refusal (`material-change-outside-its-authority`).
- **Editorial (D2, CON-003).** The effect function returns `carried: 'editorial'` and gives each later step the outcome `carried` (published) or `unchanged` (not run yet): no step is reset, kept by rule E8 or asked "still holds", the Story stays where it was, and nothing waits for an acknowledgement. Approval writes the amended wording and records the scope revision as for any amendment; the revision's stale set is unchanged. The application says `carried: 'editorial'` with the steps it carried, the summary says it too, and the history entry says the evidence of the reworded clauses was carried. Evidence evaluation carries each step's evidence of those clauses across that revision through the amendment's approving decision (`editorialCarriers`, beside the "still holds" and plan-row carriers), labelled "approved it as editorial, so it is carried", and the approve screen lists it as "Carried as editorial" (AC-014).
- **Found: settling and editorial.** An amendment that only updates the scope to what a conformance report found (SCS:REQ-022) keeps its "still holds" rows whatever its class: the report shows the code differs, so the rows are the check.
- **Surfaces.** The preview (terminal, `--json`, and the agent view within its 4,000 bytes), the decisions card on every host and the Copilot CLI card show the same words: "How big: a refinement. AC-001 has a bound test (test/value.test.mjs) [tested-clause]; AC-001 is an acceptance criterion [acceptance-clause]." and "Who decides: …". The card offers each stricter class as its own approval ("Approve AMD-001 as a material change"), held to the groups that decide that class, with its exact terminal command; never a lower one. The hosts render the card's lines and options unchanged.
- **The chat entry (REQ-015, AC-015).** `/sf-amend`, and `/sf-reject`'s intent-amendment step, say the preview's summary in plain words (what changes, its size and why, what is redone or kept, who decides), ask "Propose it?", and propose only on the person's Yes, with their own words as the reason; deciding and acknowledging stay on the Decide card or in their own terminal. The yes is bound to the person's own answer as a "still holds" row's is (`copilot-answer-binding.mjs`): on a Story with change impact on, the contract computed at refresh carries `policy.changeImpact`, and `story intent-amendment propose` in a /sf-reject or /sf-amend turn is an answer-bound step, the only one held there. It stands only on the reply to a question that asked whether to propose it, never on another yes or one typed ahead. A host's auto-reply, a declined question, a no or Not now proposes nothing, and each refusal says what to do in the same turn. No new mutation route, stop, exit or hand-off sentence. The guard reads the flag from the turn's contract, so its closure stays at 68 modules.
- **Found: the /sf-amend turn's reminder gave it the wrong job.** A paid Copilot CLI run on main said "approval-only turn … Approval/rejection only … end this turn after the decision. Draft an intent amendment only at the issued file …, for story intent-amendment propose --file." A model whose skill body did not load copied that command without its binary, was refused twice and gave up. An /sf-amend turn is now an "intent-amendment turn" whose rules name its route with full commands (draft at the issued file, preview, propose after the person's bound yes where change impact is on; a person decides and acknowledges). /sf-reject keeps "approval or rejection only" and its draft sentence names the full propose command; the paused step's rules, an Epic source note's and a no-Story draft turn's name theirs with the binary too. `test/cic-chat-entry.test.mjs` pins both summaries and checks that no turn summary names an SFlow command without its binary.
- **Found: VS Code's own chat may never hand the hook an answer.** There a proposal cannot be bound to a yes, so the refusal says not to ask again in that chat and gives the person's own terminal command (`--authored human`), as a "still holds" row there goes to the card.
- **Churn and reversals (REQ-016, AC-016).** The preview already said which changed clauses the Story changed before and which go back to an earlier statement (stage 2). The Story report (`sflow report`, Markdown and HTML) now has "Requirement changes" under the switch: the approved amendments by class, each clause changed more than once, and each clause that went back to an earlier statement, with how often. No limit is set (D4).

### 7.7 What stage 7 built (the counts), and what building it found

- **One read, model-free (REQ-017).** `src/change-impact-counts.mjs`, loaded only by the Story report (`sflow report` and the report read), computes the counts from the Story's records. For each approved amendment and for the Story it counts:
  - revisions by class (no size where change impact is off);
  - cone size and widened share;
  - evidence kept;
  - "still holds" questions and answers;
  - steps reset, and the generations they published again;
  - publications refused.

  Then it counts the Story's churn. The report's Markdown and HTML have a **Change impact** section in place of stage 6's "Requirement changes", whose clause lines it holds, and `--json` carries the same object as `changeImpact`. No command family was added.
- **What each count is.**
  - *Cone size:* the amendment's stale clauses (changed, or depending on a changed one), and its affected later steps that had published work when it was approved. A step that never published holds no evidence (REQ-006), so a code or convergence step not yet run is not counted.
  - *Widened share:* of those steps, the ones whose only reason was `unresolved-record`: in the cone only because what they depended on is not fully recorded (REQ-007's `widened:unresolved`). `unreadable` (REQ-020) is its own reason and is not counted as widened.
  - *Evidence kept:* the approval's preserved evidence items; for an editorial change, the evidence items of the steps it carried.
  - *Steps reset:* the steps the approval did not keep that had started: they had published a generation by then, or their pin set aside unpublished work (switch 2). A step that had not started reads the amended scope anyway, which is no rework.
  - *Generations redone:* the generations each reset step published after the approval, up to the next amendment that reset it again.
  - *"Still holds":* each row the amendment asked (D1's `prompt-held` rows and SCS:REQ-022's), answered "still holds" or "redo", still open, or no longer asked because its step moved on.
  - *Publications refused:* each `phase publish` refused with `PHASE_DRAFT_PREDATES_AMENDMENT`. A prepublish blocker is a check before publication, not a refusal at it, and is not counted.
  - *Churn:* clause changes, clauses changed by more than one amendment, and the times a clause went back to an earlier statement.
- **Derived first.** Cone size, steps reset, generations redone, churn and the class come from records the Story already keeps: the amendment summary (stale clauses, affected and kept steps, preserved evidence, class, rows), the scope revision it recorded (each later step's generation then), the steps' generation publications and pins, and the answers. Without a scope revision, a step's generation then comes from its publication times.
- **Recorded, additive (D3, REQ-019).** Three facts had no record:
  - The approval's `impactCounts` (in the application and the summary) holds the steps reached only through an incomplete record (switch 1), the reset steps whose unpublished work their pin set aside (switch 2), and the evidence an editorial change carried (switch 3). A later amendment's pin replaces a step's own, so the set-aside part is not derivable afterwards. It is written only when a switch is on; its presence says the counts are kept for that amendment.
  - `refusedPublications` holds one `{ amendment, phase, generation }` per refused publication. A refusal rolls its publication back, so the phase command records it afterwards as a draft of the Story's state, as `phase begin` keeps a generation, and the step's next governed commit carries it. Recording never changes the refusal.
- **Recorded or derived, said per count.** Each count names its source: recorded, derived, not recorded, or, for a total, partly recorded.
  - A Story whose amendments were approved before stage 7 reports everything it can derive. Per amendment it names what it did not record: the widened share under switch 1, the publications refused under switch 2, and the evidence an editorial change carried.
  - With every switch off nothing is missing: no step can be widened, no publication can be refused, and the amendments have no size.
  - It never fails: a record it cannot read counts as absent, and if counting fails the section says so.
- **No content.** The counts hold counts and amendment IDs only; the records hold step IDs and generations. The section names clause IDs only where stage 6's "Requirement changes" did (switch 3's churn lines).
- **Found (E5, the stage 7 real Story): two kinds of generation have no prompt by design, and read `receipt-missing`.** On the paid run, `spec dependencies` showed the scope generations that approving AMD-001 and AMD-002 published, and the kernel-assembled verify generation, as `unresolved (receipt-missing)`.
  - Neither was model-authored from an unrecorded prompt. The scope generation is the proposal its approvers decided, against the scope it amends (a moved proposal is refused at approval). The kernel assembles a deterministic document from its governed inputs record, with no model call.
  - The effect never read the scope step's own records, so those widened no cone. A kernel-assembled document cites every clause it lists, so it was in the cone for `cites` anyway, and the counts count a step as widened only when `unresolved-record` is its only reason. They still read as unknown dependencies, and a document that did not cite a changed clause (an added one) was widened.
  - The fix, under switch 1 (`promptlessInput`, `src/dependency-records.mjs`): such a generation's record takes the file it read as its receipt, bound by digest. That is the proposed bytes of an approved amendment (`receipt.kind: intent-amendment`), or the governed inputs record of a document whose pinned generation policy is deterministic, published as deterministic with no model call (`governed-inputs`). Its input edges are every clause the steps before it define at the scope revision current at publication, exactly what a composed prompt's clause capsule holds.
  - Unchanged: a code step's code still needs its prompt receipt, even when its summary is deterministic, and so does every generation a model or a person wrote. Such a generation without one reads `receipt-missing` as before.
  - Records written before the fix are not rewritten (REQ-019).
- **Found: an acknowledgement sets `acknowledgementRequired` to false.** "Reset nothing" is therefore read from the record as: approved as editorial; or not acknowledged and not required; or rows from a conformance settlement.
- **Tests.** `test/cic-change-impact-counts.test.mjs`:
  - exact counts on a synthetic Story with three amendments, and its pre-stage-7, all-off and malformed readings;
  - a real Story through the CLI with change impact on (an editorial change, a refinement partway through the code step, one refused publication): its `report --format json` counts are exact, and its Markdown and HTML sections say them;
  - an all-off Story's report;
  - the E5 fix on a real Story: the two amendment scope generations and the kernel-assembled verify resolve, the design a person wrote again without a prompt stays unresolved, and a third amendment (an added clause) widens only that design.

### 7.8 Stage 7: change impact on by default (owner decision 11, 2026-11-15)

- **The default.** `normalizeSpecPolicy` reads an unset `spec.changeImpact` as `on`, so a new Story pins `changeImpact: on` and `observedDependencies: on`, as it did when a repository wrote the setting. `off` is the opt-out, and it is now pinned as `off`: a policy normalized again (the work type's merge over the repository's, and the Story's resolution) must not read an opted-out workflow as unset and turn it on.
- **Existing configurations keep loading.** A workflow that wrote `spec.observedDependencies: off` and no change impact had turned switch 1 off before the default, so it opts out of change impact too. A work type does the same over its repository (`workTypeSpecPolicy`), and then inherits neither the repository's observed dependencies nor its scope-change authority. Only an explicit `changeImpact: on` with `observedDependencies: off` is refused; the refusal names both ways out. `changeImpact: off` with `observedDependencies: on` stays switch 1 alone.
- **Seeded workflows get it from the code, not the file.** First built by writing `changeImpact: on` into `templates/workflow.yml` (which `sflow init` copies and every seeded workflow reads) and into each new workflow from the Studio or `workflow define`. That was withdrawn the same day. Refresh pushes the packaged workflow into each registered repository's configuration, and an older build refuses a `spec` field it does not know (`spec contains unknown field 'changeImpact'`), so every teammate on it could neither start a Story nor refresh. The template, the shipped example and new workflows therefore write nothing, and the template's GDP lock digest is unchanged. The default reaches them through `normalizeSpecPolicy`.
- **Shown where a person looks.** `changeImpactSetting` (`src/change-impact-setting.mjs`) says the effective value and where it comes from: "Change impact: on (default)", "(set by the repository)" or "(set by this workflow)". Workflow Studio's model has it for the repository and each workflow, and the VS Code Studio page shows it in a workflow's settings. `workflow check`, its JSON and the builder's check print it per workflow, and `spec dependencies` says it for a Story.
- **Running Stories never change (REQ-019, D3).** Every reader checks the pinned raw field (`resolution.spec.changeImpact === 'on'`), and a Story's accepted closure supplies its pinned policy unchanged, so a Story pinned before the field reads every switch off. `spec dependencies` says when change impact is off and how it was turned off.
- **Found, and decided (decision 12, "signed rows win").** Under the default a separate plan a person wrote without a composed prompt has an incomplete record, so an amendment that carries its rows reset it anyway (`phr-amendment-plan-rows`, owner decision 4 of PHR). Now a plan whose carried rows are signed (`carriedRowSigning`, `src/amendment-plan-corrections.mjs`) is not widened. That means a counted signature under one of the plan's own approval groups, as the evidence it carries reads it, or, before approval, rows still to be signed. The approval records `impactCounts.carried: [{ phase, reason: 'signed-plan-rows' }]`. The preview and the card say the plan keeps its approval once its approvers sign the rows, then that they signed them. A signature that does not count (another group, or one SF Guard does not verify) and a plan with no carried rows are widened as before.
- **Tests.** `test/cic-change-impact-default.test.mjs`: the packaged and seeded configuration has no `changeImpact` key and every seeded workflow resolves on; nothing set is on, and `off` or `observedDependencies: off` alone loads with it off, a work type too; a Story pinned without the field reads every switch off; a new workflow writes nothing, and Studio and `workflow check` show where its value comes from; a new Story pins it on, and an opted-out Story keeps its policy byte for byte when the setting changes. The stage 2 to 6 fixtures that prove baseline behaviour, or switch 1 alone, now set `changeImpact: off` explicitly. `test/iac-decide-card.test.mjs` checks the card under the default for an editorial, a refinement and a material change, and `test/phr-amendment-plan-rows.test.mjs` walks decision 4 with change impact on and off, an uncounted signature and no carried rows.

## 9. Owner decisions, 2026-11-13

1. **D1, the clause in the prompt.** A document step that rule E8 would keep because it does not cite the changed clause, while that clause was in its prompt, is asked "still holds" by its approver on the decisions card: one question per such step per change (REQ-021). Built in stage 4, behind switch 1. Until then the preview shows "was in its prompt" as information beside "kept: cites none".
2. **D2, editorial changes.** An `editorial` change (wording only: no bound test, no acceptance clause, no expected-path or test-binding change; rules bound the class, a model may only propose it) carries evidence without per-step "still holds". The scope approver approves the new wording as editorial, and the record says it was carried that way (REQ-013, REQ-014).
3. **D3, additive records.** Records are additive: `scope-revision/v2` beside v1, no factory reset and no migration that rewrites a record. SDS:REQ-144 is amended accordingly (REQ-002, REQ-019).
4. **D4, no default allowance.** No default allowance limit. The preview and the report show churn and reversals; measure first (REQ-016).
5. **Publication of superseded work** is refused, not rebased (REQ-009).
6. **Naming.** The classes are `editorial`, `refinement` and `material`. The preview is `story intent-amendment preview`, beside the existing `draft` and `propose`. The scope-change authority is a new optional policy key naming an `approvalAuthorities` group, defaulting to the scope step's own approval groups.
7. **Older Stories.** The widening of REQ-006 applies only to Stories whose pinned policy enables switch 1; a Story pinned before enablement keeps baseline behaviour (REQ-019).

## 9a. Owner decisions, 2026-11-14

1. **Stage 4 takes the recommended defaults** (section 7.3).
2. **Supporting changes count as covered when the plan lists them.** A changed path named as a supporting change in an approved plan row or approved plan amendment (`PAM-NNN`, add-supporting) counts as covered and depends on no clause; any other changed path no clause claims stays `unclaimed-paths`, and the record is unresolved (REQ-005).
3. **A "still holds" row may be answered yes or no in chat.** This covers the D1 rows (`reason: prompt-held`) and the older SCS revalidation rows alike.
   - Yes is "still holds" (`unchanged`); no is "redo", which returns the Story to that step. Each answer is the row's own `upstream-issue revalidate` command, run by the /sf-decide turn for the bound Story only.
   - The chat answer binding holds the command to the person's own answer. A host's auto-reply, a declined question, or a reason they did not type is refused.
   - The command must also match the yes or no they gave for that row's question: Yes runs `unchanged`, No runs `return`. A mismatch, no yes or no, or Not now records nothing, and the refusal says to ask again.
   - One yes or no carries one row. A departure is held the same way (yes accepts, no rejects). An applicability or a plan row whose question names it is held to its Yes, since its No records nothing.
   - A reason shorter or longer than the command takes is refused before the command runs, with "ask the person for a reason of at least N characters", so the turn goes on. The question states the minimum.
   - The question names its row and the recommended answer and why, in one line. The row is never settled for the person.
   - Only the step's own approvers may answer; the command checks this as before. `decide-amendment`, a forward amendment's own approval, stays the approvers' own terminal step.
   - Where SF Guard requires the Story's person steps, no row offers chat: this row, and every other row whose chat choice SF Guard signs. Every host says the row is recorded in the person's own terminal.
4. **Stage 5: a draft from before an approved amendment is refused, not rebased.** The check is bound to records: the scope revision digest a code generation and a composed prompt started under, never times. Its way out is in the same step: begin and compose the step again. It names its exact commands, loops nothing back and stalls no other step. Switch 2 is on for new Stories (`spec.preAmendmentDrafts: refuse`, pinned), and a Story pinned before keeps main's behaviour. When the step is redone, the recomposed prompt names the old draft read only, at its exact path, marked as written before the amendment, to keep only where it still holds (section 7.4).
5. **A reopened document's text may stay as it was.** When the author of a document step the amendment reopened finds that the old text still holds under the amended scope, it is published unchanged; nothing makes them edit it to get past a check. What stays required is the record: an agent's draft is composed again under the amended scope, and the step's approvers approve it again.
6. **A document step reopened by an amendment is not stalled by a later code step's unfinished code; that code stays for the code step** (2026-11-14). The code is recorded at the closure and left out of the document step only while its bytes match; the code step picks it up when it begins again (section 7.5).
7. **Stage 6: the class floor is set by rules.** Registered, model-free rules compute the lowest class a change can have (`editorial`, `refinement` or `material`) from the clause diff and its bindings, following REQ-013 exactly, each with its reasons as rule IDs and plain words. The agent or the proposer may only suggest a stricter class: a suggestion below the floor is raised to it and the preview says why, never refused for that (AC-013). A person can raise the class: the proposer when proposing, the scope approver when deciding, on the Decide card or in their own terminal. Nobody goes below the floor. The record keeps the floor, the suggestion, each raise (who, from what, to what) and the effective class (section 7.6).
8. **Material changes are approved by the scope step's approvers by default.** The optional key `spec.scopeChangeAuthority` names an `approvalAuthorities` group that approves a material change instead. A material approval by someone outside that authority is refused with `INTENT_AMENDMENT_MATERIAL_AUTHORITY`, which names who decides it and how, in the same step.
9. **The chat entry.** In chat the agent drafts the change and runs the preview, shows the person in plain terms what changes, the class and why, what is redone, what is kept and who must confirm, and asks yes or no. Only after the person's own yes does it propose, through the existing route (`/sf-amend`, and `/sf-reject intent-amendment`). Approval and acknowledgement stay on the Decide card or in the person's own terminal. No new mutation route, no new stop, and no new exit or hand-off reason in any skill. The yes is bound to the person's own answer, as decision 3 binds a "still holds" row: a host auto-reply or a declined question proposes nothing.
10. **Switch 3 turns on with switch 1, as one visible setting:** `spec.changeImpact: on | off`, default off (stage 7 decides the default; decision 11 sets it on). On, the Story pins switches 1 and 3 together. `spec.observedDependencies` stays valid on its own. Switch 2 is unchanged. A Story pinned before has neither and keeps baseline behaviour with no record rewritten (REQ-019, AC-018); with everything off, behaviour matches main (AC-017).

11. **Change impact is on by default (2026-11-15), after the E5 fix, and the seeded workflows say so** (the owner's words: "yes, turn it on by default after the fix and seeded ones also"). A workflow that does not set `spec.changeImpact` runs with it on; `off` is the opt-out. The default is applied in code and written into no configuration: a new field in what init and refresh distribute would make a teammate's older SFlow build refuse the whole configuration (an upgrade analyst reproduced it, 2026-11-15). Workflow Studio, the builder, `workflow check` and `spec dependencies` show the effective value instead. A Story already pinned keeps its behaviour, and nothing rewrites a pinned policy (REQ-019). A repository that wrote `spec.observedDependencies: off` and no change impact keeps loading, with change impact off (section 7.8).

12. **Signed rows win (2026-11-15).** When an intent amendment carries a separate plan's rows, and an approver of that plan signed them in their own decision on the amendment (PHR:REQ-008), the plan keeps its approval: the signed rows are the plan's own answer, so an incomplete dependency record (REQ-005, REQ-006) does not widen it. A step whose rows were not carried and signed is still affected, fail closed. The approval records such a plan as carried (`impactCounts.carried`, reason `signed-plan-rows`), not widened, and the preview and the Decide card say so before approval (section 7.8).
